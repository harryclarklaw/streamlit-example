# Briefing Format — Horizon Engine

The output format for all Horizon Engine strategic briefings.

---

## Format Specification

```
================================================================================
HORIZON BRIEFING — [Date in format: DD MONTH YYYY]
================================================================================

Sectors:  [comma-separated list of configured sectors]
Markets:  [comma-separated list of configured geographies]
Scan ID:  [unique identifier for this briefing run]

================================================================================
IMMEDIATE — Action Required (next 2–4 weeks)
================================================================================

► [DEVELOPMENT TITLE — ALL CAPS]

  What happened:
  [Two sentences maximum. Factual. Cite the specific regulatory body, legislation,
  or enforcement action. Include the date of the development.]

  Strategic significance:
  [Two to three sentences. Commercial implications only — not legal analysis.
  Focus on: competitive dynamics, market access, cost structure shifts, or
  first-mover opportunities. Be specific about which market participants
  benefit or lose.]

  Who should care:
  [Specific client types, sectors, or business models affected. Not generic
  categories — name the operational profile that is exposed.]

  Smart move:
  [One to two sentences. Concrete, actionable. What would a well-informed
  board member want to know, and what would they instruct their team to do
  this week?]

  Source: [URL or source reference]

---

[Repeat for each IMMEDIATE item]

================================================================================
EMERGING — 3–6 Month Horizon
================================================================================

► [DEVELOPMENT TITLE — ALL CAPS]

  What happened:
  [Same format as above]

  Strategic significance:
  [Same format as above]

  Who should care:
  [Same format as above]

  Smart move:
  [Same format as above]

  Source: [URL or source reference]

---

[Repeat for each EMERGING item]

================================================================================
DIRECTIONAL — Strategic Signals (12+ months)
================================================================================

► [DEVELOPMENT TITLE — ALL CAPS]

  What happened:
  [Same format as above]

  Strategic significance:
  [Same format as above]

  Who should care:
  [Same format as above]

  Smart move:
  [Same format as above]

  Source: [URL or source reference]

---

[Repeat for each DIRECTIONAL item]

================================================================================
CALIBRATION NOTE
================================================================================

[Only include after 5+ refinement cycles]

Based on your feedback, this briefing is weighted toward:
- [Topic/sector/geography receiving increased attention]

And suppressing:
- [Topic/sector/geography receiving decreased attention]

Tell me if this needs adjustment.

================================================================================
END OF BRIEFING
================================================================================
```

## Formatting Rules

1. **Urgency classification is mandatory.** Every item must be categorised as IMMEDIATE, EMERGING, or DIRECTIONAL. If uncertain, classify as EMERGING.

2. **Section ordering is fixed.** IMMEDIATE first, then EMERGING, then DIRECTIONAL. Empty sections should display "[No items in this category for this scan period]".

3. **Item count targets:**
   - IMMEDIATE: 1–3 items (high bar — must genuinely require near-term action)
   - EMERGING: 3–7 items (the bulk of most briefings)
   - DIRECTIONAL: 2–5 items (long-range signals that inform strategic positioning)

4. **Tone requirements:**
   - Write as a McKinsey partner briefing a CEO
   - Confident and commercial — no legal hedging
   - Forward-looking — focus on what comes next, not just what happened
   - No phrases like "firms should monitor this space" or "this may have implications"
   - Every item must contain a concrete "so what" and "now what"

5. **Source attribution:**
   - Every item must include at least one source link
   - Prefer primary sources (government, regulatory body) over secondary analysis
   - If multiple sources corroborate, cite the most authoritative one

6. **Calibration note:**
   - Only appears after 5 or more refinement cycles
   - Briefly summarises the weighting adjustments
   - Invites correction to prevent filter bubbles

7. **Length targets:**
   - Full briefing: 800–2,000 words
   - Individual items: 100–200 words each
   - Prioritise density over comprehensiveness — better to have 8 sharp items than 15 generic ones
